Other ways to sign: a key you keep outside the browser

Two keys on your own machine: your root (the key production trusts, shown in your card below) and a signing key your root certifies once. sign_digest.py (Python 3 with the cryptography package) does all of it. Make the signing key, certify it with your root, then sign the digest; the last line submits the signature and the certificate for you instead of pasting them:

python3 sign_digest.py keygen --out ~/{{ user.party }}.key
python3 sign_digest.py certify --root ~/{{ user.party }}-root.key --role {{ user.party }} --signing-key "$(python3 sign_digest.py pubkey --key ~/{{ user.party }}.key)" > ~/{{ user.party }}.cert
python3 sign_digest.py sign --digest {{ view.digest }} --key ~/{{ user.party }}.key
python3 sign_digest.py sign --digest {{ view.digest }} --key ~/{{ user.party }}.key --certificate ~/{{ user.party }}.cert --party {{ user.party }} --user {{ user.username }} --submit {{ request.base_url }}a/{{ aid }}/sign/upload

Without --submit, paste the signature it prints and the certificate file's line into your card below under Paste a signature and press Verify and store. With OpenSSL 3 instead (on a Mac, brew install openssl, the built-in one cannot do this), one command signs the digest with a PKCS#8 signing key file; the certificate still comes from sign_digest.py certify:

printf 'AIAB/plan-bundle/v1\0{"plan_digest":"%s"}' {{ view.digest }} > /tmp/aiab-msg && openssl pkeyutl -sign -inkey ~/{{ user.party }}.pem -rawin -in /tmp/aiab-msg | base64

Development keys from make dev-keys are already on file: the signing key is ~/.aiab-dev/{{ user.party }}.key (.pem for openssl), its certificate ~/.aiab-dev/{{ user.party }}.cert, the root ~/.aiab-dev/{{ user.party }}-root.key.
